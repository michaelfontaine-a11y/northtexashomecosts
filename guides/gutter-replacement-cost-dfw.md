New gutters in Dallas-Fort Worth typically cost between **$4 and $16 per linear foot installed** in 2026, which puts a common 150-200-foot single-story home in the **$1,200-$3,000** range for seamless aluminum — the material most DFW homes use. The spread is wide because material choice does most of the work: budget vinyl sits at the low end, seamless aluminum in the middle, and steel, zinc, or copper climb well above it. Height, roof pitch, gutter size, and whether old gutters need tear-off after a hailstorm push totals up from there.

This guide breaks down 2026 DFW gutter pricing by material and by component, explains what makes North Texas different (hail, sudden downpours, slab-and-attic construction), and offers practical advice on sizing, guards, and insurance. Figures reflect 2026 cost data from national aggregators (HomeGuide, Angi, Forbes Home/Fixr) cross-checked against typical DFW exterior-contractor pricing; treat them as honest ranges, not quotes. ## Gutter cost by material (2026, installed)

| Material | Typical cost per linear foot (installed) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vinyl (sectional) | $3 - $6 | Cheapest; can crack/warp in extreme heat and cold |
| Aluminum (seamless) | $6 - $14 | Most common in DFW; rust-proof, custom-cut on site |
| Galvanized / painted steel | $9 - $20 | Stronger, more hail-resistant; can eventually rust |
| Stainless steel | $18 - $40 | Very durable, premium price |
| Zinc | $20 - $40 | Long-lived, low-maintenance, high upfront cost |
| Copper | $25 - $45 | Premium look and lifespan; specialty labor |

These are installed prices for standard K-style residential gutters. Half-round profiles and oversized 6-inch gutters run higher within each material.

## What drives the price in North Texas

Several local factors make DFW gutter pricing different from the national average:

- **Hail and severe storms.** North Texas sits in one of the most hail-prone regions in the country, and DFW is repeatedly among the costliest metros for hail claims. Large hail dents and perforates aluminum and vinyl gutters, and wind-driven debris tears down runs and downspouts. Damage often happens alongside roof damage, which is why gutters are frequently replaced as part of a storm claim. Steel gutters resist dents better than aluminum but cost more upfront.
- **Intense, bursty rainfall.** DFW storms tend to dump a lot of water in a short window rather than a steady drizzle. Standard 5-inch gutters can overflow in these bursts, which is why many local contractors recommend upsizing to **6-inch K-style gutters with 3x4-inch downspouts** — a modest per-foot premium that moves substantially more water and reduces overflow onto foundations and fascia.
- **Expansive clay soil.** North Texas's shrink-swell clay expands when wet and contracts when dry, and poor drainage right next to the house accelerates that cycle — a leading contributor to foundation movement locally. Properly sized gutters plus **downspout extensions or splash blocks** that carry water 4-6+ feet away from the slab are one of the cheapest forms of foundation protection you can buy. Skipping them undercuts the whole point of the gutters.
- **Slab-on-grade homes with attic HVAC.** Most DFW homes are single-level slab construction, which keeps many gutter runs at a workable height and helps keep aluminum jobs affordable. Two-story homes, on the other hand, carry a meaningful height-and-safety labor premium.
- **Heat and UV.** Long, hot summers are hard on vinyl, which can grow brittle, fade, and warp over years of sun exposure. That's a big reason seamless aluminum — which won't rust and holds up better to UV — is the DFW default despite costing a bit more per foot.
- **Roof pitch and single-story sprawl.** Ranch-style DFW homes often have long, low rooflines that are quick to work, but cut-up rooflines, steep pitches, and lots of inside/outside corners add fittings and labor regardless of material. Because roof pitch and area drive both gutter and roofing labor, it's worth pricing them together — our [roof cost calculator](/tools/roof-cost-calculator/) can help you scope the bigger picture before you call contractors.

## Choosing gutters (and guards) wisely

- **Default to seamless aluminum unless you have a reason not to.** It's rust-proof, custom-cut on-site to reduce leak-prone joints, and priced in the middle of the market. Step up to steel if hail resistance is a priority, or to copper/zinc for longevity and looks on a higher-end home.
- **Size for DFW rain, not the catalog default.** Ask whether 6-inch gutters and 3x4 downspouts make sense for your roof area and pitch. The small upcharge is usually worth it here.
- **Don't skimp on downspout drainage.** On clay soil, where the water exits matters as much as the gutter itself. Budget for extensions or buried drains that discharge well away from the foundation.
- **Match gutter guards to your trees, not the sales pitch.** Micro-mesh and reverse-curve systems ($ at the top of the $6-$14/ft range) handle fine debris and pine needles best; simple screens or foam ($ at the bottom) are cheaper and fine for light leaf loads. Guards reduce cleaning but don't eliminate all maintenance — be skeptical of "never clean again" claims.
- **Get itemized, per-foot quotes.** A good estimate lists linear feet, material and gauge, gutter and downspout size, number of downspouts, tear-off, and any fascia repair separately. Vague lump sums make comparison shopping impossible.
- **Ask what's warrantied and for how long.** Separate the material warranty (often long on the metal itself) from the workmanship/labor warranty (frequently just 1-5 years). Leaks usually come from installation, so the labor term matters. Get it in writing.

## Storm damage and insurance in North Texas

Because hail and wind do so much of the gutter damage here, insurance is often part of the story:

- **File promptly and document everything.** After a hailstorm, photograph dents, detached sections, and downspout damage — and note that gutters and roofs are commonly damaged in the same event. Texas has deadlines for filing claims, so don't wait.
- **Know your deductible.** Many Texas policies carry a separate **wind/hail deductible of 1-2% of the home's insured value**, which can be a few thousand dollars — sometimes more than a modest aluminum gutter job by itself. Price the repair before assuming a claim is worthwhile.
- **Use licensed, local contractors and beware storm-chasers.** After big DFW hail events, out-of-town crews flood the area. Verify the contractor is established locally, insured, and willing to put scope and warranty in writing before you sign anything or hand over an insurance check. ## Bottom line

For most Dallas-Fort Worth homes, **seamless aluminum gutters at $6-$14 per foot** are the practical, durable default, landing a typical single-story project around **$1,200-$3,000** installed. Budget vinyl saves money upfront but rarely holds up as well to Texas heat and hail, while steel, zinc, and copper buy extra durability or curb appeal at a higher price. Whatever material you choose, size the system for North Texas downpours, route the water well away from your slab, and — after a storm — document damage and check your deductible before filing. The cheapest gutter is the one that actually protects your foundation and doesn't have to be redone after the next hailstorm.
